Question 01

Tell me about a reliability incident you handled.

“A deployment increased error rates for one service. I confirmed the impact, rolled back, and told the incident channel what we knew and what we did not know yet. We then traced the cause to a configuration change, added a pre-deploy check, and documented the rollback. The lesson was to treat configuration changes with the same review as code.”

Why it works: it shows ownership, clear communication, a concrete control, and a learning loop.

Question 02

How do you balance reliability with delivery speed?

“I use an error budget. If the service is within its target, we can keep shipping. If the budget is exhausted, we pause risky changes and spend time on reliability. The tradeoff is explicit, so the team argues about evidence rather than opinions.”

Why it works: it names a mechanism, a decision rule, and the tradeoff.

Red flags
  • Answering with definitions only, without an example.
  • Blaming people or systems without explaining your action.
  • Claiming you have never seen an incident.
  • Avoiding the tradeoff between speed and reliability.
